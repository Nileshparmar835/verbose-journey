## What determines the look

- **Staging.** A narrow field of view usually contains one action and a recognisable prop. Faces and objects cluster around the lower middle. A comparatively quiet region above them holds short, often single-word captions. The samples include dark and warm interiors, pastel skies and green daylight.
- **Drawing.** Rounded animal silhouettes, dark outlines, flat colour regions, soft background gradients and shaded frame edges carry the look. Small props remain identifiable at phone size. The people use simple bodies with expressive eyes and hands.
- **Camera.** Repeated wide compositions establish geography. A closer face, prop or symbolic insert changes the question. Motion can stop after the critical action so the viewer can see what changed.
- **Captions.** Small light words appear in a predictable band near the upper third. Selected words change colour. The volume and cue-sheet analysis concerns the supplied media. The original voice model, animation software and source project cannot be identified from the encoded files.
- **Retention logic.** A strong first image raises a concrete question. Repetition gives the viewer a prediction, changed evidence creates tension, and the human example pays off the prediction. Audience retention was not supplied for these references. These are editorial observations.
