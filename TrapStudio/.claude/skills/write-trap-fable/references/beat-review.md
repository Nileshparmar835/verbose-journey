# Beat review

| Moment | Editorial test | Repair |
|---|---|---|
| First frame | Can a muted viewer identify the animal, desired object, and immediate contradiction? | Open on a missed meal or a closing door. Move backstory later. |
| Second attempt | Has the animal received different evidence? | Change a condition the audience can see, such as another animal taking the accessible food. |
| Turn | Can a viewer explain why the character insists in six words? | Replace a lecture with a choice between two objects. |
| Human scene | Does the human face the same decision under different circumstances? | Map lost digging time to an already-paid ticket. Give the next hour a visible clock. |
| Last image | Does the opening object acquire new meaning? | Bring the original prop back and change what the character does with it. |

For a different plot, start with a new pair of objects, a new setting and a different cost. For example, a moth circling the brightest shop sign and a shopper choosing the biggest sale label could explore attention capture. Establish that the prominent option changes their choice before naming a bias.

If metrics are supplied, separate the hold at the first frame from drop-off at the explanation. A hook experiment can change just the opening image and line while holding the rest fixed. Label projections as hypotheses.
